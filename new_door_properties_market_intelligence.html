MOVEMENT 3 · THE GAP

What the data is telling us

ANTIMONY INSIGHT

What we see that others miss

New Door is outperforming on results and underperforming on infrastructure. $9.3M from three agents, 182 reviews at 5.0, 80% of GCI concentrated in two people. These are the numbers of an office at a specific inflection point: strong enough to scale, fragile enough that it has to. The brand and digital infrastructure are the missing piece on both sides of that equation.

⚡

UNFAIR ADVANTAGE?

The New Door Foundation and Gurjant's 11 years of community involvement have built pre-appraisal trust in Bonner, Throsby, Moncrieff and Taylor that no competitor can fast-track. This is a vendor acquisition engine most agencies would need a decade to replicate, and almost none of it is visible in New Door's current marketing. In a market where educated ACT buyers research thoroughly before making contact, community depth like this changes the shortlisting conversation before it starts.

✦

SILVER BULLET?

Yash and Abhi are producing at 2.6x the ACT independent average with no dedicated brand infrastructure behind them. That same infrastructure — proper agent profile systems, listing campaign assets, a consistent content calendar — is also what attracts the next generation of high-performing agents to New Door. In the ACT's independent-dominant market, agents choose offices based on brand authority and the tools available to them. Building the ecosystem that makes Yash and Abhi more visible to vendors simultaneously makes New Door more attractive to the agents who would reduce that 80% GCI concentration and push the office toward $14–16M.

🎯

LOW-HANGING FRUIT?

At 56 days median DOM versus the ACT franchise average of 53, the gap is narrow but visible to vendors doing comparison research. A clear narrative that reframes New Door's community-driven, high-touch approach as the deliberate reason vendors choose them converts a potential comparison weakness into a positive selection signal. The story already exists in the data. It is not currently being told.


MOVEMENT 4 · THE RECOMMENDATION

What we would do for New Door

OUR APPROACH

Three priorities · One direction

Based on what the data shows, we would focus on three things. Not simultaneously, in sequence, each one building on the last. The detail lives in the conversation, but the direction is clear.

1

Build a brand that matches the production

New Door's agency review profile (182 reviews, 5.0) and team GCI put it squarely in the top tier of independent offices in Canberra. The website, social presence and listing campaign aesthetics have not kept pace. A brand system that visually communicates the authority this office has earned would immediately change how vendors perceive New Door at the research stage, before they contact anyone.

ELEVATE YOUR BRAND

2

Build the infrastructure that attracts agents, not just vendors

80% of New Door's GCI sits in two people. The path to $14–16M is adding three agents at even half Yash and Abhi's productivity — but in the ACT's independent-dominant market, agents choose offices based on brand authority and the tools available to them. The same personal brand infrastructure that makes Yash and Abhi more visible to vendors — dedicated profile pages, consistent listing campaigns, a content calendar — is exactly what makes New Door more attractive to the next high-performer looking for a home. One build. Two compounding returns.

SCALE THE TEAM

3

Push median price up through the Googong corridor

New Door's Googong presence (15 properties, second highest suburb by volume) sits in a $990k median house market but the office median is currently $855k. A deliberate campaign targeting the $950k–$1.1M segment across Googong, Denman Prospect and Whitlam would lift median price without geographic expansion. The gap between current performance and prestige positioning is smaller than it appears.
